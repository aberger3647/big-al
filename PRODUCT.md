# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People looking for **personalized online strength and conditioning coaching**. They want expert guidance to get stronger, fitter, and healthier, but many feel intimidated by traditional gym culture or unsure where to start. They arrive on a phone or laptop, often after seeing a recommendation or social post, sizing up whether this coach is someone they can trust and relate to. The job to be done: understand the training philosophy, see proof it works (testimonials, blog content), and take the first low-friction step toward coaching (join the waitlist).

## Product Purpose

A marketing site for **Big Al Strength & Conditioning**, an online personal training and coaching business. It exists to convert curious visitors into coaching inquiries by showcasing the coach's philosophy, real client success stories, services and rates, and a regularly updated blog. Success is a visitor who feels confident and welcome enough to join the waitlist.

## Brand Personality

**Energetic, friendly, encouraging.** High-energy and athletic without being aggressive. The tone is that of a coach who genuinely wants you to win: direct and motivating, but warm and approachable. It celebrates progress, lowers the intimidation barrier, and makes strength training feel achievable for everyday people. Voice is plainspoken and human, never clinical, never bro-y, never corporate.

## Anti-references

This should NOT look or feel like:

- **Corporate big-box gym chains** (Planet Fitness and similar): impersonal stock photography, salesy, templated, soulless.
- **Hardcore bro / supplement-ad aesthetic**: black-and-neon, shirtless-flex intimidation, aggressive supplement-store energy.
- **Clinical wellness / spa**: soft pastels, minimal calm-wellness tone that reads passive, medical, or low-energy.
- **Generic SaaS landing page**: cookie-cutter startup template with a gradient hero, identical feature-card grid, and rounded-everything blandness.

## Design Principles

1. **Energy you can feel, intimidation you can't.** The design should pulse with athletic momentum (bold type, confident color, decisive layout) while staying warm and welcoming to beginners.
2. **Proof over promises.** Lead with real client stories and tangible content. Show the results and the thinking, don't just claim them.
3. **Human coach, not a brand machine.** Keep the voice and imagery personal and specific. The visitor is choosing a person to train with, so the personality of the coach should come through.
4. **One clear next step.** Every screen should make the path to joining the waitlist obvious and low-friction. Never make the visitor hunt for how to get started.
5. **Committed, not decorated.** The bright palette and heavy black borders are a deliberate identity. Use them with conviction, not as scattered accents.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AAA**. Hold body and UI text to AAA contrast ratios (7:1 normal, 4.5:1 large) against their backgrounds, which demands care given the bright yellow/teal/blue/orange palette (pair light brand colors with black text or dark surfaces, not white-on-light). Full keyboard operability with visible focus states, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and never rely on color alone to convey meaning. Inclusive, non-intimidating language that welcomes beginners and all body types.
